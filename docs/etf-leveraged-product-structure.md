# ETF and leveraged-product structure

The ETF primary-market view compares three common structures without claiming
current holdings for a specific ticker. A traditional index ETF commonly uses
an asset basket and cash adjustments in its creation/redemption process.
Leveraged and inverse ETFs can instead use swaps, futures, short positions,
other derivatives, and cash or collateral to pursue a stated **daily** target.

Most leveraged and inverse ETFs reset that exposure each trading day. Their
returns over periods longer than one day therefore compound from daily returns
and can differ significantly from the stated multiple or inverse of the
benchmark's return over the same period. Exact instruments, weights, costs, and
reset mechanics remain product-specific and must be checked in the fund's
prospectus and current holdings disclosure.

The UI links the SEC's general ETF creation/redemption bulletin and its updated
2023 leveraged and inverse ETF bulletin. It does not provide investment advice,
a live holdings feed, or an expected-return forecast.
